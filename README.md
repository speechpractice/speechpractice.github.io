# Speak & Move

Free daily voice, speaking and gentle movement practice for adults, including people living
with Parkinson's, stroke, multiple sclerosis, brain injury or an aging voice. Instructions in
English, voice practice in 11 languages.

**Open the app:** https://speechpractice.github.io/

It started as an app for my mom. It's free for anyone who can use it.

## What it does

The app has two sections, switched with tabs at the top.

### 🏃 Move

- **Circuit format.** A warm-up, then the main exercises in a circuit, twice (once in the
  short-session style), then a cool-down, moving on with one tap. Each finished exercise gets a ✓.

- A short daily routine of gentle exercises: marching, wide reaches, sit to stand, heel raises,
  knee straightening, side steps, supported balance, punches, a steady walk, stretches and
  calm breathing. Each one has a **seated** version and a **standing** version done holding a
  sturdy chair or counter.
- **Seated and standing mix.** With the seated routine on, the main exercises alternate between
  the seated and the supported standing version, so over two rounds each one is done both ways.
  Every exercise page has a Seated / Standing switch, and each version gets its own ✓ once
  done today. After a "yes" to falls or unsteadiness, seated stays the default, and standing
  is offered only with someone beside you.
- **Animated demos.** Each exercise shows a looping stick-figure animation of the movement,
  matched to the seated or standing version. It holds still when the device asks for reduced motion.
- **Safety check first.** Four yes/no questions. A "yes" about medical supervision, chest pain
  or faintness, or a recent stroke, heart problem, surgery or injury locks the section until
  the person confirms their doctor has said gentle home exercise is okay. A "yes" about falls
  or unsteadiness switches to the seated routine.
- The practice style shapes the routine (larger movements and rhythm, both sides, short and
  cool sessions, steady pace, or strength and balance), drawing on published guidance such as
  the Parkinson's Foundation/ACSM exercise recommendations and the WHO 2020 guidelines for
  older adults.

### 🗣️ Speak

- **One tap from exercise to exercise.** The held-voice exercises (long, high and low "Ahh")
  rotate as a circuit, one round each: 1, 2, 3, 1, 2, 3 … until all rounds are done (5, or 3 in the
  short-session style). Counting, Pa-Ta-Ka, phrases and reading then follow once each. Rotating
  practice tends to help older adults retain skills, while the fixed daily order keeps it simple.
- A checklist strip on every exercise screen shows ✓ for finished exercises and round progress
  (for example 2/5). Leaving mid-session and coming back continues where you stopped.

- **Spoken coach tips.** After each round, a short spoken tip based on how much of the round
  reached the loudness target (for example "Great, nice and loud!" or "Take a big breath first,
  then go louder"). It is worked out on the phone from the loudness meter: no recording, no AI
  service, nothing sent anywhere. It comments only on loudness and never changes the plan. It
  can be turned off in Settings.

- Users can choose a practice style. Styles adjust pacing and the number of rounds; they are
  not treatment plans. The table shows each style and the people who often choose it:

  | Style (often chosen by) | Daily plan | Why this style |
  |---|---|---|
  | Loud voice (Parkinson's) | Long "Ahh", high and low slides, counting, Pa-Ta-Ka, phrases, reading (about 20 min) | Loudness practice has the strongest evidence for soft Parkinson's voice |
  | Slow and clear (after stroke) | Long "Ahh", slow Pa-Ta-Ka, paced counting, slow and clear phrases, paced reading (about 15 min) | Loudness plus rate control and clear articulation |
  | Breath and pacing (brain injury) | One breath per sentence, long "Ahh", slow Pa-Ta-Ka, slow and clear phrases (about 15 min) | Breath-group control, loudness and rate |
  | Short and easy (MS) | One breath per sentence (3), long "Ahh" (3), 6 phrases (about 10 min) | Short sessions to avoid fatigue, with breath support |
  | Strong, easy voice (aging voice) | Long "Ahh", high and low slides, phrases, reading (about 15 min) | Sustained tones, pitch slides and strong functional speech |
  | General | The full set (about 20 min) | |

- Everyday phrases come from a pool of 30 per language: 4 favorites every day plus 6 that
  rotate daily (or the same 10 every day, in Settings)
- Seven short exercises (about 20 minutes): long "Ahh", high and low slides, counting,
  Pa-Ta-Ka, everyday phrases, and reading aloud
- A live loudness meter that uses the phone's microphone, because people with a soft voice
  often feel loud when they aren't
- Big buttons, large text, and instructions read aloud
- Progress tracking: day streak, days per week, and longest "Ahh" per day
- The app is in English. On first open, people choose the language they want to practice:
  English, Chinese (Mandarin), Hindi, Spanish, Arabic, French, Bengali, Portuguese, Russian,
  Indonesian or Tamil. Phrases and reading practice use that language, with English meanings,
  and they can add the English phrases too. Non-Latin scripts can show in English letters
  (for example "Mujhe ek cup chai chahiye."), the original script, or both. People can add
  their own phrases.
- Private: no sign-up and no cookies. Names, phrases and practice history stay on the device.
  The public site counts anonymous visits, finished exercises and the chosen practice language
  with [GoatCounter](https://www.goatcounter.com), with no cookies and no personal data. It
  never records names, phrases or practice style.

Add it to the home screen from the browser menu to use it like an app.
